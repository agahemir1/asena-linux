## Panel

By default the panel runs along the bottom of the screen. The start button (an eight-pointed star) and your apps are on the left; the tray, status icons and clock are on the right.

- Apps you pin always stay on the panel.
- A running app gets a short line beneath it; the window in front gets a wider line in the accent color.
- To move the panel to the top, left or right, see [Personalization](/en/docs/personalization/).

## Start menu

Click the star or press <kbd>Super</kbd> + <kbd>Space</kbd>.

- The top row holds your account, Settings, lock and power.
- Below are your pinned apps, and below them every app under letter headings. Click a letter heading to open a grid of the alphabet and jump to any letter.
- The search box sits on the edge facing the panel. It finds apps and settings; a settings result opens the right section of Settings.
- Right-click an app to pin it to the menu or the panel.

## Control center

Click the status icons at the bottom right or press <kbd>Super</kbd> + <kbd>A</kbd>.

- Volume and brightness sliders, with a picker when there is more than one audio output.
- Wi-Fi, wired and Bluetooth rows. Each has a switch, and network and device lists open in place.
- Buttons for do not disturb, dark style, night light and power profile.
- Battery status, and buttons for a screenshot, Settings, lock and power.

Hardware the computer does not have, such as a battery or Bluetooth, simply has no row.

## Notifications and calendar

Click the clock or press <kbd>Super</kbd> + <kbd>N</kbd>. A full-height panel slides in from the right: the day and date, a calendar, a media card for whatever is playing, and all your notifications.

## Desktop icons

Your home folder and the contents of your Desktop folder show as icons on the desktop. Double-click, or press <kbd>Enter</kbd>, to open one.

- Right-click empty space for: new folder, open in terminal, open in Files, show desktop icons, change background, display settings, desktop and panel settings.
- Right-click an icon for: open, rename, move to Trash.

## Windows

Windows have minimize, maximize and close buttons. <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> switches between them. Windows fade briefly as they open and close; you can turn animations off in Settings › Accessibility.

## Screenshots

The <kbd>Print</kbd> key saves the whole screen to Pictures › Screenshots and shows a notification. For a delay or a selected area, open the Screenshot app.

## Lock and session

The lock button in the menu’s top row locks the screen. Automatic locking and sleep when idle are off by default; turn them on in Settings › Power.
